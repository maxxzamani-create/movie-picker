What's actually built

One box. It's called
the SBX-1.

Not a deck slide, not a mockup. A physical box that sits between a venue's cable or streaming box and the TV. It watches and listens to the picture right there on the device — no schedule from the network, nobody feeding it cues — and it only takes over during a real commercial break. The second play resumes, the screen is back on the game.

Why the break is the opening

The screens are the venue's.
The breaks aren't.

A venue pays for the TVs, the programming, the wall they hang on and the power they burn. Then the game cuts to a break and those screens go to work for somebody else. That's the whole opening, and it's not a small one — roughly a third of an average NFL broadcast is commercial time, and there's peer-reviewed work in the Journal of Marketing showing that promoting an item on in-venue screens raises the chance a customer buys that item, with the effect strongest for indulgent items, new items, weekends, later in the day, and crowded rooms.

BreakShark gives the venue its breaks back. The venue is the customer. No ad network sits on the venue's walls, and every slot in the break points at the venue's own food, drinks and events.
