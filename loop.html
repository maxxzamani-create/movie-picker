Why a screen is worth doing right

The research here is closer to your room than to a sports bar.

The strongest peer-reviewed measurement of what an in-venue screen actually does was run in retail — not in bars. Which makes it a better fit for a salon, a showroom, or a waiting room than it is for the sports side of this business, and we'd rather say that than oversell either one.

+8.1% higher probability that a customer buys the specific item that's on the screen — from a peer-reviewed field experiment in the Journal of Marketing covering 237 campaigns and roughly 30 million shoppers, matched against what people actually bought. The effect ran stronger for new items, stronger later in the day, and stronger when the room was busier. Every figure and its caveats →
Read it straight, including the part that doesn't flatter us. That study found screens changed which items people bought — not how much they spent overall. So the honest pitch for Loop mode isn't "this conjures new money." It's that the thing you most want moving is the thing on the screen, all day, in front of people who are already sitting in your room with nothing else to look at. And it was measured in a store, not in your salon, so treat it as the mechanism rather than a promise about your week.

Straight answers

What owners ask about Loop.

  • Do I need cable for this?No. That's the whole point of Loop mode — there's no broadcast involved. The box plays your channel on its own. If you ever do add live TV later, the same box starts catching commercial breaks automatically, no new hardware.
  • Who changes it when my prices change?Text me. That's the current answer and I'd rather be honest about it than pretend there's a portal. Venues running a box also get a dashboard where they can reorder and swap what's playing from a phone.
  • Is it going to look like a cheap slideshow?It shouldn't, and if it does, tell me and I'll rebuild it. The spots are built from your actual photos with real type and motion — the same production that goes on the sports side, pointed at your services instead of somebody's wings.
  • Can it hear my customers?No. There is no microphone and no camera on the box. It has no physical way to see or hear your room — it reads a cable signal, and in Loop mode it isn't even doing that. The full description is here →
  • What does it cost?A Loop Week is free with nothing to sign. Beyond that we're not making pricing claims on this site and we're not going to invent a number for a webpage. When it's set it'll be here in plain English, and nothing gets billed unless you've seen it and said yes to it.
  • Are Loop venues an afterthought to the sports bars?Fair question, and the honest answer is that the sports side is louder because break detection is the harder engineering problem and it's what the test record is about. But two of the ten founding spots are held specifically for Loop venues, and they're held on purpose — a waiting room that runs this well is a better proof of the product than another bar.
