Advertising doesn't stack in a straight line — the second time somebody sees your wings during a game does less work than the first, and the sixth does less than the fifth. Straight answers

What owners actually ask

Does it ever interrupt the game?

No. Your broadcast is never changed, delayed, or interrupted. The BX-1 only changes what your screen shows during a commercial break that's already happening — and the game is back the moment play resumes. In the frame-by-frame test it actually came back about 5 seconds early, during the channel's bumper.

What happens when it misses one?

Nothing. The break plays like it always has — the game runs straight through the box. On streaming it's caught 62 out of 62 in testing. On satellite it's caught 2 of 4, and we say so right on the page. Worst case is the TV you already own.

I've got 14 TVs. Do I need 14 boxes?

One box covers one cable or streaming box. If six TVs are all showing the same cable box, one covers all six. Rooms with a bunch of different feeds are exactly what a Catch Night is for — we walk your setup and give you the honest count before anything else happens.

What do you need from me to start?

Photos of your food, your drinks, and your room. Founding venues get 3 professional spots made from those photos, and you run the lineup from your phone.

Is this even allowed?

Your TVs, your room, your call. The BX-1 never touches, records, or rebroadcasts the signal — it just changes which input your own screens show during a commercial break, the same thing you do when you grab the remote. Venue screens have been run this way openly for years across the country. And if you ever want it off, the bypass remote kills it instantly.

Why isn't there a price on the site?

Because we're not making any pricing claims right now, and we're not going to invent a number for a webpage. When pricing is set it'll be here in plain English — and nothing gets billed unless you've seen it and said yes to it. A Catch Night costs you nothing either way.
