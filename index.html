How it works

Four steps. Then it runs itself.

The BX-1 wires in behind the setup you already own and works with the provider you already have. The only thing that changes about your night is whose food is on the screens during the break.

A small box sits between one receiver and every TV showing that game. Six games at once means six boxes; two games means two. Satellite, cable or a streaming app — it reads the picture, so it doesn't care which.

Questions

The things owners actually ask.

Does this mess with my TV provider?

No. BreakShark never alters or rebroadcasts the channel. While we are still training it, it keeps stills of the TV picture — never the room — so we can check its calls, and you can see them any time. It reads the picture coming down your own line and switches which input your TVs show — the same thing a person with a remote does, done faster and automatically. It runs on DirecTV for Business today, and on cable and streaming boxes. If your provider wants it in writing, ask and we'll put what it does and doesn't touch on paper for them.

What if it ever sits out a break?

Nothing happens. The break plays like it always has and the game runs straight through the box. Across the four graded sessions it touched thirty seconds or less of play per hour outside a commercial, and every figure on this page traces to a hand-graded file.

What does it cost?

Pricing is set with the Founding 10, and we'd rather say that than invent a number. Spots you create in Ad Studio carry a small per-spot cost on top of the plan; anything already in your library plays free and unlimited.

What happens to the sound?

Most bar TVs run muted, and the box matches that: your ads run silent by default, so the room sounds exactly like it did a second earlier. If a screen carries sound and you want your ads to as well, that is a switch we set at install, per feed.

Can my staff override it?

Always. There's a kill switch, and the box stands down whenever someone is driving the TVs by hand. It's built to lose that argument on purpose.

Is it watching my customers?

There is no camera and no room microphone in the product. It sees the television line and nothing else, and it records nothing. What leaves the building is a heartbeat — up or down, breaks caught, which of your promos is playing — and one still of the TV picture so your dashboard can show what the box is looking at. Never audio, never the room. The full description →

I've got 14 TVs. Do I need 14 boxes?

No — screens are never what gets counted. One BX-1 sits on one cable or streaming box and covers every TV showing that feed. What you count is simultaneous games: three different games at once means three feeds, so three boxes. Tell us how many games you run at once on a Saturday and we'll give you the count before we come out. Restaurant groups: same text, we'll scope the whole group.
