Competition

NOT ONE RIVAL — A CATEGORY. WE OWN THE EMPTY ROW.

The industry splits into four models. Two make the venue the product (advertisers pay). Two sell the venue a cost (content or signage). None turn the commercial break inside the live game into the venue's own revenue.

AMBIENT AD TV

Atmosphere · Loop
Free channels on your other TVs. Advertiser pays; you're the inventory. Never the live game or its breaks. ($1B / ~50K venues; Loop delisted 2024.)

BREAK AD NETWORK

Taiv
Our mechanic, sold to advertisers. Venue keeps 1 of 6 slots + ~$1–2K/yr; all six = enterprise-only. Proved the market, capped the upside.

ENGAGEMENT / SIGNAGE

UpShow (now EverPass)
Decorates your other screens — trivia, social walls, promos — and can rent them to brands. Ignores the break. ~30K screens.

CONTENT / RIGHTS

EverPass · DirecTV
Sells you the game itself (~$10K/season est.). A complement we sit on top of — their ~300K venues are our best leads.

BREAKSHARK — THE EMPTY ROW

Venue-paid. Game stays on. All six in-game slots are yours. Zero third-party ads. The only model where the break sells the venue's own menu.

Path to profit: venue-paid SaaS is cash-positive at ~105–440 venues — under 2% of U.S. sports bars. No need to match Atmosphere's ~50,000. And every content-player venue is a pre-qualified lead.

Moat & legal posture

WHY WE DON'T GET SUED AND OTHERS DO

  • We never touch the broadcast signal. BREAKSHARK changes what the screen displays during a break — legally equivalent to muting the TV or flipping inputs, which bars have always done. No retransmission, no signal modification.
  • Detection is on-premise. The box reads pixels and audio levels locally; nothing is recorded or redistributed.
  • The data becomes the moat. Every installed screen builds a fingerprint library of local break patterns → detection accuracy compounds and follow-on entrants start from zero.
  • The network is the endgame. Installed screens become sellable local ad inventory — the Atmosphere playbook, plus live sports.
