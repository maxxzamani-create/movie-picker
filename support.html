The thing nobody asks until it's too late

What happens to your TVs if BreakShark goes away.

Fair question for a pre-launch company with one guy in it. The answer is built into the hardware, not into a promise.

Your game feed does not pass through our software. The broadcast runs through the box on its own path. If our software crashes, if the box loses power, if the company disappears entirely and nobody ever updates anything again — the break just plays like it always has, and your TV is the TV you already owned. There is no scenario where a BreakShark failure leaves you with a dark screen or a bill for a repair. That is a deliberate design choice and it's the first thing I built.
IF THE BOX DIES

The break plays normally

The feed passes straight through. You lose the takeover, not the game. Text me and I ship a replacement — you don't pay for hardware that failed on its own.

IF YOU WANT OUT

How many boxes

One box per feed. TVs don't count.

The single most common question, and the one most likely to be answered wrong by guessing. The rule is simple, and it's about your cable boxes — not your screens.

THE RULE

One box, one feed

A BX-1 sits on one cable or streaming box. It catches the breaks in whatever that feed is showing.

YOUR SCREENS

Hang as many as you want

If eight TVs are all showing the same cable box, one BX-1 covers all eight. Screens are never the thing being counted.

DIFFERENT GAMES

Each simultaneous game is its own feed

Three different games on at once means three feeds, so three boxes. Two games, two boxes. It tracks your cable boxes exactly.

Don't take my count over the phone. Big rooms get this wrong in both directions — I've seen fourteen-screen bars that genuinely need two boxes, and six-screen rooms that need four. That's the whole reason a Catch Night starts with me walking your setup and giving you the honest number before anything else happens. If the honest number is bigger than you hoped, you'll hear it from me at the walkthrough, not on an invoice.
